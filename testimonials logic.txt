● Generally yes — most real websites use static or semi-static testimonials, not live database pulls. Here's how it actually works across different types of
   sites:

  ---
  Static (most common)

  Hardcoded in the frontend like yours. The team manually picks 6-10 strong quotes, writes them in, and they stay. 99% of landing pages do this — Coursera,
  Udemy, Duolingo all have handpicked static testimonials on their marketing pages.

  Why: Testimonials are curated marketing content, not a live feed. You want control over what appears.

  ---
  CMS-driven (mid-tier)

  Testimonials stored in a database/CMS (like WordPress, Contentful, Sanity). The marketing team can add/edit them without touching code, but they're still
  manually curated. The page fetches them on load.

  ---
  Real reviews (advanced)

  Platforms like Udemy or Amazon pull actual user reviews — but only after filtering:
  - Minimum star rating (e.g., 4+ stars only)
  - Flagged for featured display by an algorithm or admin
  - Usually cached, not real-time

  ---
  For SkillSphere

  Your platform already has course ratings/reviews from students. You could make testimonials "real" by:
  1. Adding a featured: true flag to the reviews table in your DB
  2. An admin marks good reviews as featured
  3. Landing page fetches GET /reviews?featured=true

  But honestly for now, static is fine and standard. Real platforms only switch to dynamic testimonials when they have thousands of reviews to draw from.
